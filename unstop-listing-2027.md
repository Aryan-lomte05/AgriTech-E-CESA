## About

**24 hours. One real farm problem. One room full of people trying to solve it.**

Most hackathons ask you to build another app for people like you. This one asks you to build for the people who feed the country. Drones that spot crop stress. Rovers that spray with precision. A voice assistant that answers a farmer in their own language. If you can build it, there is probably a field that needs it.

Agri-Tech Hackathon is a national-level, 24-hour offline sprint hosted by K. J. Somaiya School of Engineering, Mumbai, and organised by [[e-CESA]]. Software and hardware builds are both welcome.

**Last edition, in numbers and names**

- 1,772 registrations on Unstop
- ₹2,00,000 prize pool
- First place: Team Snack Overflow, D. J. Sanghvi College of Engineering, for an automated tractor-mounted sprinkler system
- Partners included [[BKT, LIC, NABARD, Festo, KIAAR, Red Bull and Domino's]]

**Why register**

- The prize pool is now ₹3,50,000, up from ₹2,00,000 last edition.
- Registering is free, and Round 1 is one short abstract you can write from anywhere.
- You don't need an agriculture background or prior AI, IoT or blockchain experience.
- Stuck for an idea? Pick an official problem statement or bring your own under Open Innovation.
- Mentors stay with you during the sprint, and the organisers provide food and snacks.
- You pitch your build to a panel of industry and academic judges.

## Notes for the committee (do not publish)

**Fixed from last year's listing**

- It had no email or phone number, only Unstop's query link. Contacts are now included.
- Its eligibility tags included postgraduates, but its About section said undergraduates only. Pick one. This draft uses both because it reaches more people.
- Its tags were Business Plan, Coding Challenge and Entrepreneurship, with nothing about agriculture or hardware.
- It had a 2.5/5 rating from only 2 users. Ask real participants from last year to leave honest reviews on the new listing once it is live.

**Check these numbers**

- Unstop shows 1,772 registered. The website says 1,200+ participants and 300+ teams. The draft uses Unstop's figure because it is the public record. Correct the website or the draft so the two agree.
- Confirm that last year's partners are happy to be named, and drop any who aren't.

**Ideas to make it crazier (each needs a committee decision)**

These are ordered by how much I expect them to help, which is judgement rather than measured data.

1. **Campus ambassadors with a leaderboard.** Give one student per college a personal link and a prize for the most sign-ups.
2. **Ask Unstop for promotion.** They are listed as a partner on the site, so ask what placement that can include. I could not find public details on featured listings or pricing. Their blog lists coffee@unstop.com.
3. **A Farmer's Choice Award.** Real farmers vote on the most useful build. No generic hackathon can copy this, and it makes a strong headline.
4. **More than cash.** Add internships, incubation or pilot deployment with a sponsor, or a prize for each theme, on top of the ₹3,50,000 pool.
5. **Lower the entry barrier.** Allow team changes until the abstract deadline, and accept a 1-page abstract or short deck. Then update Rules and Rounds above.
